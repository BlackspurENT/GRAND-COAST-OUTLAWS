# GRAND COAST OUTLAWS — GAME VISION

> **Creative direction and proposed systems — not a list of implemented features.**

## Enter the other side of paradise

Grand Coast Outlaws imagines an open-world multiplayer action game set in a fictional coastal region. The intended experience sits at the intersection of police operations, criminal ambition, civilian travel and a city that reacts to player choices.

A long-term **30 × 30 km (900 km²)** map is a proposed scale target. A small coherent district and a reliable multiplayer loop come first. Visual concepts do not establish that any target map size or landmark has already shipped.

## Regions & atmosphere

The intended world combines a dense downtown, beachfront neighborhoods, docks and industrial areas, highways and elevated routes, more remote terrain and islands. Nighttime city lighting, changing ambience and contrasting luxury and working districts give the setting its identity.

A military or restricted-use complex and an airfield have appeared in concept explorations; their content, role and final design remain unconfirmed.

## Player paths

**Police:** identify incidents, patrol, pursue suspects and coordinate a response.  
**Outlaws:** escape capture, plan robberies, seek rewards and evade a rising level of attention.  
**Other city roles:** travel, earn money, interact with businesses and participate in the broader world as systems mature.

These paths describe creative goals rather than finished playable professions.

## The intended core loop

1. Enter a shared city and select an initial gameplay path.
2. Travel to an objective through the local district.
3. Attempt a playable police or criminal activity.
4. Trigger a meaningful response from the opposing role or simulated world.
5. Resolve the encounter and update the player's reward/risk state.
6. Repeat with better coordination, movement and tools.

The first completed loop should be demonstrated in a **small vertical slice** before the world expands.

## Planned points of interest

Potential objective locations include regular banks, a larger national bank, jewelry shops, retail stores, a police headquarters, a prison complex and cultural sites. No single location is claimed here as already realized in a final-quality world.

## Experience principles

**Player agency:** meaningful alternatives across police and criminal gameplay.  
**Readable systems:** clear UI, map, minimap and transparent objective feedback.  
**Multiplayer first:** validate connected independent clients and replication early.  
**Atmosphere:** a coherent coastal setting, realistic aspirations, and strong visual identity.  
**Evidence over hype:** document a working feature before calling it complete.

## Technical approach

Unreal Engine 5 and C++ are the selected core technology. Editor prototypes and small-scope local tests are the publicly documented foundation. The next engineering gates are a small reproducible playable district, standalone server packaging, remote connectivity and performance profiling.
